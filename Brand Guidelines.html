Brand Guidelines · Grothouse · v1.0

One family. Twenty-four voices. Same bones.

The Grothouse Family is a multi-entity design system. Every company, product, and initiative across the family speaks with its own accent while sharing the same skeleton — typography, layout rhythm, radiance language, motion grammar. This document is the source of truth for the visual and behavioral rules that make that possible.

02Principles

What this brand holds to be true.

Five principles guide every decision — from the width of a hairline rule to the pace of an aurora drift. If a design choice violates one of these, the design is wrong, not the principle.

01 · Editorial, not marketing

Grothouse writes like a publication, not an ad. The default hero is a headline and a single sentence, not a value prop stack. Serif display type, generous whitespace, and a quiet radiance — never loud gradient marketing.

02 · Light is the default

All components, templates, and marketing surfaces are designed light-first. Paper-white backgrounds, ink-on-paper text, a single accent for emphasis. Dark mode exists and is fully supported, but as a user preference — not as the brand's native voice.

03 · One accent per surface

Every entity owns exactly one accent color from the blue family (hue 210–260°). That accent appears on at most 8% of any surface — in italic display type, micro-rules, a single button, an orb glow. If you want two accents, you are on the wrong entity or the wrong surface.

04 · Radiance is atmospheric, not decorative

The aurora mesh, orb glow, and noise texture are the family's signature — a soft gravitational pull toward the accent. They are atmospheric: they occupy the background only. Never put radiance in front of content. Never increase it to compete with copy.

05 · Archetype before entity

Each entity inherits its motion speed, border weight, and corner radius from its archetype (Holding, Financial, Real Estate, AI/Tech, Consumer, Charitable, Platform, Consulting). The archetype decides how the entity behaves; the accent decides how it looks. Both must agree.

03Logo System

Three marks. Four variants each.

The Grothouse identity is built around three logo marks at different scales of expression. Each mark ships in four variants — Dark, Light, Black, and White — to cover every surface, medium, and contrast condition.

The three marks

When to reach for each

  • Wordmark — the default for anything representative of the brand: letterhead, annual report covers, site header, press releases, signage. If in doubt, use this.
  • Monogram — constrained square surfaces: favicons, app icons, avatars, social profile images, physical ID badges, embroidery.
  • Tech — digital-native and developer-facing surfaces: API docs, terminal outputs, dev portal, engineering blog, product UI chrome.

Variants & usage

VariantSurfaceWhen to use
DarkDark backgrounds (#0B0E14 and near-black)Default for dark-theme digital surfaces, dark reports, dark video backgrounds.
LightLight backgrounds (paper, #F0F2F5 and lighter)Default for print, stationery, light-theme digital — the primary variant in most contexts.
BlackSingle-ink print (one-color)Newspaper, fax, legal documents, embroidery, engraving — any single-ink reproduction on light.
WhiteSingle-ink print reversedDark apparel, dark signage, video watermarks, single-ink reversed on dark.

For all four variants across all three marks, see the Logo Package.

Clearspace & minimum sizing

  • Clearspace — at least one "x-height" of the wordmark's cap height on all four sides. For the monogram, one full inner-ring diameter. No other graphic, photo, or text may enter this zone.
  • Minimum size (digital) — Wordmark: 120px wide. Monogram: 32px square. Tech: 140px wide.
  • Minimum size (print) — Wordmark: 24mm wide. Monogram: 8mm square. Tech: 28mm wide.
  • Favicon — always use the Monogram, never the Wordmark. Generate 16/32/48/192/512 PNG variants from logos/01-monogram/dark.svg or light.svg depending on surface.

Misuse

✕ Don't
Recolor the mark. The accent is always cobalt (or the entity's accent). Never use brand-foreign colors.
✕ Don't
Rotate, skew, or stretch. All marks are locked to their published proportions.
✕ Don't
Add a drop shadow, outer glow, or bevel. The mark does not wear effects.
✕ Don't
Place over busy photography without an at-least 20%-opacity scrim behind the mark.
✕ Don't
Substitute a different serif or sans for the wordmark. The type is drawn, not set.
✕ Don't
Use the monogram as a decorative pattern, tiled, or faded into backgrounds.
✓ Do
Use the SVG files from the Logo Package for all digital and print reproductions.
✓ Do
Pick the variant that matches the surface's luminance — Light on paper, Dark on near-black.

Entity accents — all in the blue family

Every entity owns exactly one accent. All 24 accents live in the blue family (hue 210–260°) for consistency. Each accent has a dark-mode variant (used on dark surfaces) and a light-mode variant (shifted ~10% darker for proper contrast on white).

Contrast & accessibility

  • Body copy — --g-fg on --g-bg is audited to AA on all 5 surface tones in both themes.
  • Accent on white — All 24 light-mode accents are darker shifts of their dark-mode counterparts specifically so accent-on-white text meets AA for 14px body type.
  • Accent on accent — never. Don't put accent-colored text on a tinted accent card; use --g-fg-strong instead.
  • Italic serif display — has a lower effective contrast; always use --g-fg-strong for the surrounding upright setting to compensate.
05Archetypes

Eight behaviors. One grammar.

Each of the 24 entities inherits its behavioral DNA from one of eight archetypes. An archetype sets how the entity moves, what shape its corners take, and how the radiance breathes. Two entities in the same archetype feel like cousins; two entities across archetypes feel like a family, not strangers.

Choosing an archetype for a new entity

Read the character line for each archetype and pick the one that most closely describes how the entity should feel to a customer. If two feel close, pick the one with the more restrained motion — restraint is always on-brand.

07Voice & Tone

How the family speaks.

The visual system is only half the brand. The way Grothouse writes is the other half. Editorial, sober, quietly confident — the voice of a family office that has already won and doesn't need to sell you on it.

Tone spectrum

The family voice sits near the editorial end of the spectrum — closer to a long-form quarterly letter than a product page. Short paragraphs. Full stops. A period between the weight-bearing word and its modifier. Built on faith. Driven by purpose.

✓ Yes
  • "A diversified portfolio of operating companies, held across generations."
  • "Twenty-one years of compounding. Twelve operating entities. One family."
  • "We invest where stewardship outlasts cycles."
  • Understated numbers. Precise commas.
  • Italic serif for the weight-bearing word.
✕ No
  • "We're transforming the future of [category]!"
  • "Unlock the power of..." / "The leader in..."
  • Exclamation points. Emoji. Superlatives.
  • Acronyms without expansion on first use.
  • Hero copy that reads like a pitch deck bullet.

Per-archetype voice modulation

  • Holding — authoritative, generational. Sentences are short, declarative. Use we sparingly.
  • Financial — precise, numerate. Every claim carries a figure. No rhetorical flourishes.
  • Real Estate — grounded, physical. Reference place, tenure, craft. Materials named.
  • AI/Tech — electric, sharp. One technical noun per sentence. No metaphors.
  • Consumer — warm, direct, second-person. Still restrained.
  • Charitable — quiet, humble. First-person stories from participants, not the foundation.
  • Platform — infrastructural. Verbs of scale: power, connect, route.
  • Consulting — strategic, executive. One insight per paragraph, delivered with confidence.
08Applying the System

How to build on this foundation.

Any new site, product surface, or piece of collateral starts from the same four decisions. Make them in order. The rest of the design system follows.

Step 01 — Pick the entity

From the 24 in the directory, or add a new one by appending to kit/tokens.json > entities. The entity sets the accent and secondary colors.

Step 02 — Inherit the archetype

The entity's archetype field determines motion speed, border weight, and corner radius. Do not override these locally.

Step 03 — Set the root attributes

<html data-entity="grothouse_family"
      data-archetype="holding"
      data-surface="cool"
      data-glow="subtle"
      data-preset="editorial"
      data-theme="light">

Always set data-theme="light" explicitly on <html>. The kit defaults to light if missing, but an explicit declaration protects against regressions.

Step 04 — Import the kit

<link rel="stylesheet" href="kit/grothouse-system.css">
<link rel="stylesheet" href="kit/theme-toggle.css">
<!-- optional, only if you want the toggle -->
<script src="kit/theme-toggle.js" defer></script>

Step 05 — Compose with semantic tokens only

Every color, font, and radius in your stylesheet must reference a --g-* token. If you find yourself typing a hex value in the application layer, stop — either add a token to the system or reuse an existing one.

What this brand system is not

  • A component library. The kit defines tokens and primitives only. Components are the application's responsibility.
  • A CMS. Copy, imagery, and IA are authored in whatever CMS the application picks.
  • A marketing page. The Theme Playground exists to audit the system, not to sell it.
