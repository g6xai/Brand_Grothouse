Grothouse Design System

Motion

Three durations, one easing, and a hard stop for anyone who asks for less motion. The archetype speed multipliers — 1.3× for ai_tech, 0.7× for charitable, and the six others — are retired. Every entity moves at the same speed.

The scale

TokenDurationUse
--motion-fast 150ms Press feedback, hover, focus ring
--motion-standard 250ms DEFAULT — disclosure, tab switch, fade, list reorder
--motion-emphasis 350ms Sheet present and dismiss, full-screen transition
--ease-out cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1) Every transition. There is no second easing.

250ms is the default and covers most cases. Two documented exceptions: 250ms on a button press reads as lag — press feedback has to be sub-200ms to feel attached to the finger. 250ms on a full-height sheet reads as abrupt; Apple's own sheet presentation is roughly 350ms.

Feel the difference

Hover each row. If you have reduced motion enabled, nothing moves — that is correct.

150ms — fast

250ms — standard

350ms — emphasis

Reduced motion is a zero, not a softening

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce sets every animation and transition duration to 0ms and disables smooth scrolling. It does not halve the duration or swap a slide for a fade — a user who asks for no motion gets no motion.

The rule lives in grothouse-system.css and applies to every element, including pseudo-elements. Nothing opts out.

What motion is for

DoWhy
Confirm a touch landed The only feedback a finger gets on glass
Show where a thing came from A sheet rising from the bottom edge says how to dismiss it
Preserve continuity across a state change A list that reorders instantly looks like a different list

Retired with the radiance layer: the 14-second orb drift loop, the aurora mesh, the cursor-tracking card glow, and the card hover lift. An infinite decorative animation behind content contradicts deferring to that content — and it never stops, so it costs battery on every device that renders it.

Scroll is not an animation surface

No scroll hijacking. No scroll-linked animation beyond a border or shadow appearing once scrollTop > 0. Sticky headers cap at 64px.

The document scrolls — html and body stay static and visible. Pinning them stops mobile Safari collapsing its URL bar, which permanently costs about 110px of viewport and the native back, share and tabs affordances.
